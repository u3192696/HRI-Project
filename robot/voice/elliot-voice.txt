# Elliot's voice: the recipe the rung 4 lines are rendered from

Elliot is **E2**: a fresh render of a description that states the sex, then cloned for
every line. No shift was used to place him, and that is a deliberate reversal of the
method used for Owen — see §2.

## 1. The description

> A warm, natural man's voice in his mid-thirties, recorded close to the microphone in a
> quiet room. Speaking easily and unhurriedly to one person nearby, with a faint smile
> in the delivery, natural breath and small pauses. Clearly a real person, not a
> presenter.

Seed 42, cfg-scale 4, model Breeze TTS 2, in `~/Projects/breeze-tts`, rendered from
`robot/voice/intro_v2.txt` (the ladder's own script, so the measurement is comparable
with the other rungs).

Measured: **114.4 Hz, 8.4 st, ladder position 0.89.** The register is what rung 4 needs;
the 8.4 st is livelier than the ladder's own rung 4 (6.4 st), which is why Danny picked
it under a face that is smiling. Chosen by ear for character — "sounds the best, and to
my ear matches the face really well too" — with the measurement confirming it is in
band, not choosing it.

## 2. Why the description states the sex, and no candidate was shifted into place

**None of the ladder's four descriptions names a sex.** Rung 4's own is *"A natural
human voice, recorded close to the microphone in a quiet room. Conversational, warm,
calm and unhurried."* — no "man" anywhere — so the ladder's rung 4 male-ness, such as it
is, is luck of the draw.

That matters because of what happened to Milo: moving his render down 2.5 st produced
"a more deep throaty female voice instead of a deeper male voice", because `asetrate`
scales every frequency by one factor and the formant spacing that carries perceived sex
survives the transform. A shift makes the same speaker *bigger*; it cannot make them a
man. For rung 4 the same trick would have been worse than useless — a shifted render of
Milo's voice is **Milo, bigger**, and a fourth rung that sounds like a deeper Milo is
not a fourth person.

So the candidate that was chosen states the sex in its description, and the only
correction applied afterwards is a trim (1.4 st on the intro, see §3), never a placement.

Two candidates were rejected and both are worth remembering:

- **E3** scored *higher* on the ladder's composite (0.99 against E2's 0.89) while sitting
  at 158.5 Hz, level with Milo. "Quiet, reserved" produced a 3.5 st delivery and flatness
  scores as naturalness. The composite is half melodic range, so it cannot pick a voice
  on its own: read the register first.
- **E1**, the ladder's own rung 4 (114.0 Hz, 6.4 st, 1.00), was a legitimate candidate and
  lost on character, not on measurement.

## 3. The three clips, all landed 5 November

| clip | script | length | median F0 | correction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `clips/rung4-natural-human-intro.wav` | `stage4_intro.txt` | 31.7 s | 114.4 Hz | **+1.37 st** — the clone came out at 105.7 |
| `clips/rung4-natural-human-pass.wav` | `stage4_pass.txt` | 15.2 s | 114.2 Hz | none |
| `clips/rung4-natural-human-retry.wav` | `stage4_retry.txt` | 5.6 s | 113.3 Hz | none |

The anchor is **E2's own 114.4 Hz**. The intro was cloned from a 9.18 s cut of the E2
render and came out 1.4 st low, which was trimmed; the other two were cloned from a cut
of the *trimmed* intro that contains the name "Elliot", and both landed within 0.2 st
with no correction at all. That is the reason to clone from the corrected anchor rather
than from the original render — the same lesson Owen's pair taught from the other
direction.

## 4. The pass line is not only a line: it closes the run

`stage4_pass.txt` says the training is complete, that there are no more stages, and that
the phrase to finish on is the same one that started each session:

> That's it - you've completed the training. There are no more stages, and nothing else
> left to get through. Thank you for practising with me. This screen shows how you went.
> When you're ready, just say "Let's begin" one more time, and we'll finish here.

**The phrases are load-bearing.** `session.py` ends a run on its trigger phrase, so a
closing line that did not teach the phrase would leave the robot parked on the stats
screen until an operator said `standby`. It is the same reason stage 1's introduction
teaches the phrase rather than assuming it.

Measured and verified like every other clip: dry-run through `listener.py --test-file`
UNMUTED on the board, **0 events on all three** (7, 5 and 2 endpoint results), so the
robot's own "let's begin" does not fire at it. The pass ends on a clean decay to -99 dB,
not a cut mid-word — Breeze logged a tail condition on that render, so it was checked.

## 5. Rendering these three again

```bash
cd ~/Projects/breeze-tts
DESC="A warm, natural man's voice in his mid-thirties, recorded close to the microphone
in a quiet room. Speaking easily and unhurriedly to one person nearby, with a faint smile
in the delivery, natural breath and small pauses. Clearly a real person, not a presenter."

# 1. Reference: the first four sentences of the E2 render, cut on its 0.85 s pause at
#    9.18 s, with the transcript the cut actually contains.
ffmpeg -y -i output/audition/elliot-E2.wav -t 9.18 -c:a pcm_s16le output/audition/elliot-ref.wav

# 2. The introduction, cloned. Comes out ~1.4 st low; trim it onto 114.4 Hz.
./say.sh --text-file ~/Projects/hri-project/robot/voice/stage4_intro.txt \
  --instruction "$DESC" --cfg-scale 4 --seed 42 \
  --ref-audio /out/audition/elliot-ref.wav \
  --ref-text "Hello. My name is Pip, and I am a robot. I'm here to practice eye contact with you, but in a way that suits you. There are two things I'd like you to know first." \
  --output /out/audition/elliot-intro-raw.wav
ffmpeg -y -i output/audition/elliot-intro-raw.wav \
  -af "asetrate=24000*1.0823,aresample=24000,atempo=0.9240" \
  -c:a pcm_s16le output/audition/elliot-intro-final.wav

# 3. A second reference from the SHIFTED intro, containing the name, for the short lines.
ffmpeg -y -i output/audition/elliot-intro-final.wav -t 5.198 \
       -c:a pcm_s16le output/audition/elliot-intro-ref.wav
REF="Congratulations, you've made it to the last stage. My name is Elliot, and I'll be with you for this one."

# 4. pass and retry, cloned from it. Both land within 0.2 st: no second shift.
./say.sh --text-file ~/Projects/hri-project/robot/voice/stage4_pass.txt \
  --instruction "$DESC" --cfg-scale 4 --seed 42 \
  --ref-audio /out/audition/elliot-intro-ref.wav --ref-text "$REF" \
  --output /out/audition/elliot-pass-raw.wav
./say.sh --text-file ~/Projects/hri-project/robot/voice/stage4_retry.txt \
  --instruction "$DESC" --cfg-scale 4 --seed 42 \
  --ref-audio /out/audition/elliot-intro-ref.wav --ref-text "$REF" \
  --output /out/audition/elliot-retry-raw.wav
```

Then level each to **-21.3 LUFS integrated** with the gain `ebur128` reports, not peak —
see `clips/README.md`. The three arrived at -24.2, -24.6 and -26.4 LUFS, so an unlevelled
comparison would have made the retry sound worst for no reason at all.

Free the GPU first: `ollama stop gemma4:e4b`. Breeze needs ~7.7 GiB on a 12 GB card.
